# Best Cooks Club — Messaging & Differentiation Brief
### "Cognitively-grounded procedure delivery"

*First cut, 2026-06-16. Source: deep-research synthesis on presenting procedural
instructions under working-memory load (canonical findings live in this repo at
`docs/procedural-instruction-research.md`, with the long form in
`docs/procedural-instruction-research-deep.md`). This brief is the marketing
translation: it sorts what we can claim today from what needs our own evidence first.*

---

## Positioning

**One-liner (vision):** A procedure engine that re-presents any skilled job — cooking
today, repair/assembly/install next — the way human working memory can actually execute it.

**One-liner (product, today):** Recipes re-cut into memory-sized, execution-ordered steps,
designed around how people actually follow instructions with their hands full.

## The two pillars

1. **Cognitively-grounded instruction design.** We restructure procedures to fit working
   memory (~4 chunks, fewer when hands are busy), split by *element interactivity* rather
   than sentence count, let the user pace step-to-step, and adjust detail to expertise.
   Most recipe surfaces just reflow the publisher's prose.
2. **"Recipe = procedure" architecture.** A recipe is a procedure: subject + domain
   expertise + parts + tools + ordered steps + tips. The same engine generalizes to
   plumbing, assembly, and repair — a cross-domain architecture, not a recipe feature.

## Claims ledger — sort before you ship any of these

### GREEN — cite freely (established science = our design rationale)
- Working memory holds ~4 chunks (Cowan), not the folk "7±2" — so we design to a low ceiling.
- Split steps by element interactivity: independent actions apart, coupled/simultaneous
  actions together (cognitive load theory / Sweller).
- User-paced, check-to-advance segmenting is the single strongest comprehension lever (Mayer).
- Spoken instructions are transient; for hands-free use, speak the action and let the screen
  carry the numbers (transient-information effect).
- Detail that helps a novice can hinder an expert → verbose↔concise levels
  (expertise-reversal effect).
- Framing: these are our **design principles drawn from research**, not claims about our results.

### YELLOW — needs our own evidence before it's public
- "Easier to follow / fewer mistakes / faster" → an **efficacy claim about our product**.
  The science proves the principle, not our execution. Needs our own (even lightweight) user
  testing. Treat as advertising substantiation, not rhetoric.
- "Most apps just dump publisher prose" → a **competitive claim**. Needs a dated audit of the
  top N competitors on file before it goes in writing.

### RED — don't say yet
- "We are a cross-domain procedure platform." True as architecture and roadmap; reads as
  overclaim until a second domain (e.g., a real install/repair flow) actually ships. Say
  "the architecture generalizes" / "next domain," not "we are a platform."

## Proof assets
- **Have:** the verified research synthesis (incl. the verifier correctly killing the 7±2
  myth — a credibility point in itself); the live check-to-advance cook view.
- **Need:** (1) competitor audit to back the "prose dump" line; (2) a small user-outcome test
  to unlock the YELLOW efficacy claims; (3) one shipped non-cooking domain to unlock the
  platform claim.

## Known soft spots (don't build a headline on these)
- Interruption / "where was I?" place-keeping — no surviving primary sources; product gap,
  not a marketing line.
- Applied-practice lineage (Gawande checklists, Toyota TWI "key points," mise en place) —
  unverified strand; candidate for a focused second research run.

## Suggested claim sequencing
Science = provable **now** (design philosophy). Generalization = demonstrable in principle
now, provable when domain #2 ships. Outcome claims = online after testing. This keeps every
public statement defensible.
