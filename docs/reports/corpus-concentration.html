Distribution

The corpus is the long tail. The ranking truncates it.

Across 731 publishers the corpus is barely concentrated at all — 63% of them contribute a single recipe. Selecting the top 200 raises concentration eightfold and drops 95.3% of publishers entirely.

What survives is not a long tail in the sense that matters. Twelve publishers appear once each, but together they carry 6% of the list; three publishers carry a third of it. The mass sits at the head.

The finding

A ranking that reads the publisher, not the recipe

The score blends two signals: OU, how far a page outperforms what its domain would predict, and power, the domain's authority plus the page's. Both are search-authority measures. Neither has read the recipe.

That is visible at rank 10. Healthline is a general-health publisher carrying a single recipe in our entire corpus, and it lands above every page from Serious Eats, Bon Appétit and Food Network — on a domain authority of 92 it did not earn by cooking.

Healthline and WebMD have one recipe each in the corpus. Both went straight into the top 200.

A 100% hit rate, against 7% for Serious Eats and 6% for Allrecipes. The pages are real recipes, correctly extracted. The ranking simply has no way to know that a health encyclopaedia is not a cooking authority.

What follows

Two things worth doing

Cap the publisher. Any surface built on this list — a "best recipes" page, a cookbook, a digest — reads as three sites unless a per-publisher limit is applied at selection. Three publishers currently supply a third of it.

Give authority a subject. A domain-level score that knows what a publisher is authoritative about is the fix for rank 10, and it is already on the roadmap as the system-wide domain score. Until then a health encyclopaedia with one recipe outranks a cooking magazine with fifty, and the ranking cannot tell the difference because it never looks at the food.

Method

How this was measured

The blend is not a stored value — it is a percentile rank computed over whatever cohort you supply, so this report defines that cohort as the whole scored corpus. Percentiles stored on individual rows come from per-dish runs and are not comparable across the corpus.

One caveat on provenance: _scoring.power was found to be zero on 1,775 master rows whose authority figures were real, which had pinned half the corpus to the floor of the power dimension. That was repaired before this report was run, and the query below reads power derived from DA + PA rather than the stored field.

WITH ranked AS (
  SELECT source_host,
         0.70 * PERCENT_RANK() OVER (ORDER BY ou_score)
       + 0.30 * PERCENT_RANK() OVER (ORDER BY power) AS blend
  FROM master_recipes
  WHERE ou_score IS NOT NULL AND power IS NOT NULL
)
SELECT source_host, COUNT(*) n
FROM (SELECT * FROM ranked ORDER BY blend DESC LIMIT 200)
GROUP BY source_host
ORDER BY n DESC;

3,451 of 3,452 master recipes carry both signals; one row lacks them and is excluded. Figures are from recipes.db on 30 July 2026, after the power backfill.
