Who owns the top 200

Distribution

The harvest doubled the corpus. The ranking kept two houses.

Across 1,358 publishers the corpus is still barely concentrated — 58% contribute a single recipe, and the top three hold 6.4% between them. Selecting the top 200 now drops 99.0% of publishers entirely and raises concentration seventy-fold (HHI 51 → 3,753). In July the same selection raised it eightfold.

What changed is not the scoring — it is what got harvested. Since July the publisher refreshes brought in the DA-95 giants at depth: 167 new cooking.nytimes.com rows and ~100 from bbc.co.uk. Half of everything we hold from NYT Cooking now sits in the top 200. Ranks 1 through 77 contain no publisher except those two.

The finding

The July warning, compounded by the harvest

July’s finding was that the blend reads the publisher, not the recipe — a health encyclopaedia outranked cooking magazines on borrowed domain authority. That finding stands: WebMD’s single recipe is still a 100% hit (rank 115), and Healthline — which has since grown to 21 recipes in the corpus — still places one. The first publisher in the entire ranking that is neither NYT nor the BBC is Healthline, at rank 78.

The new finding is what happens when that ranking meets a deep harvest of a DA-95 site. Every NYT page inherits the same domain score, PA runs high across their catalogue, and the blend seats them in a block. The first independent food blog appears at rank 145 — thekitchn’s sauerkraut — behind 115 NYT pages, 29 BBC pages, and both health sites.

Ranks 1–77 belong to two publishers. Rank 78 is a general-health site. Rank 145 is the first food blog.

Serious Eats (102 recipes held), Martha Stewart (115), and BBC Good Food (99) place nothing at all. The pages are real recipes, correctly extracted, and the ranking is working exactly as designed — which is the problem.

What follows

The July recommendations, now overdue

Cap the publisher. July said any surface built on this list reads as three sites without a per-publisher limit at selection. It now reads as substantially one. The machinery for this exists as of this week: the per-line slot reservation seats candidates by quota inside the dish refresh — the same seating logic, applied per publisher instead of per search line, is the cap.

Give authority a subject. Unchanged and still open: a domain-level score that knows what a publisher is authoritative about (the system-wide domain score on the roadmap) is what separates a health encyclopaedia from a cooking magazine. The ranking still never looks at the food.

Method

How this was measured

Identical to July for comparability: the blend is a percentile rank computed over the whole scored corpus (raw ou_score + derived power, OU 70 / power 30). Percentiles stored on individual rows come from per-dish runs and are not comparable across the corpus; the paywall-adjusted effective_ou_score is deliberately not used here, since July predates it.

WITH ranked AS (
  SELECT source_host,
         0.70 * PERCENT_RANK() OVER (ORDER BY ou_score)
       + 0.30 * PERCENT_RANK() OVER (ORDER BY power) AS blend
  FROM master_recipes
  WHERE ou_score IS NOT NULL AND power IS NOT NULL
)
SELECT source_host, COUNT(*) n
FROM (SELECT * FROM ranked ORDER BY blend DESC LIMIT 200)
GROUP BY source_host
ORDER BY n DESC;

8,698 of 8,725 master recipes carry both signals and a source host; the rest are excluded. Figures are from recipes.db on 30 August 2026. Companion data: corpus-concentration-data.json, refreshed alongside this page.
