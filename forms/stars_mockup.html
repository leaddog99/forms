The public face of the ranking. A user sees a star fill and, for about one recipe in ten, a badge — never a number, never a second scale. Everything below is rendered from stars_demo.json, generated from the live corpus through public_scoring.public_score().

The scale

Five levels across 3–5 stars. Nothing below 3 is ever shown: every recipe in the index already passed selection, so a low star would be insulting a recipe we chose ourselves. One glyph tiled at 20% with 2 units of padding baked into each side of a 20×20 tile — that symmetry is what puts a half star exactly on a glyph centre.

Where they land

The cuts sit on the blend value, not on rank position — so a recipe keeps its stars as the corpus grows instead of being demoted by arithmetic. The blend averages two percentiles, so the spread is centre-heavy: scarce at both ends, which is what makes a top band worth having.

Ranked results — the pre-click surface

The most exposed surface, and the one a competitor scrapes. Star, badge, thumbnail, host. No ingredients, no method, no numbers.

What a user keeps. The star is frozen and dated at save time — a stored card holds the rating that was read, and a refresh surfaces a change as an event rather than silently rewriting it. The second axis is spent here, as the comparison sentence.
